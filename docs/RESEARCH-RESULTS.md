# Research Results

## Executive conclusion

The research did **not** establish a profitable strategy. Its strongest outcome is a decision record: the evidence was insufficient for promotion, so the conservative, mechanically tested baseline remained the default.

## Frozen offline screen

A fixed offline evaluation considered 1,200 candidates. Zero candidates passed every hard gate, and zero stable regions were promoted. This result rules out a broad credible region under the declared criteria; it does not prove that no strategy could ever work in another environment.

Later local diagnostics were treated only as research direction, never as a reason to weaken the frozen global decision rule.

## Short-screen finalist: reduced participation is not edge

A SELL-only research finalist was screened for 60 seconds in three controlled development runs. It produced exactly three sells in every run, reached its own `-3` position limit, and then could not participate further. An aggregate hedge could reduce desk exposure but did not restore this component's independent capacity.

| Metric | Comparator | Research finalist | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Total fills across three screens | 163 | 9 | Far less participation, not repeated signal confirmation. |
| Fills per finalist screen | — | 3 | The component saturated at its own limit. |
| Strict 500 ms eligible markouts | — | 3 | Evidence is sparse. |
| Eligible markouts | — | -2.5, -2.0, -2.0 ticks | Every eligible observation was adverse. |

The observed mark-to-market change is descriptive only. The paths were not controlled counterfactuals: changing one component can alter subsequent market evolution and scheduling. It must not be presented as causal PnL evidence.

## Recycling hypotheses: rejected on turnover and robustness

Three bounded recycling/exit hypotheses reduced saturation, but every one failed predeclared worst-case and turnover gates. Their maximum action multiples were 110.33x, 104.67x, and 102.33x relative to the reference action level.

This is a material engineering result: an apparent improvement that requires roughly 100 times more aggressive activity is not automatically a better design. Costs, market impact, operational load, and worst-case robustness matter even before considering external validity.

## Why longer runs were stopped

Longer screens would mostly extend the holding time of an already saturated one-sided position. They would not test repeated signal economics. Establishing that question would require a new, explicitly reviewed position-management or two-sided unwind design, rather than a minor parameter change.

The stop decision therefore followed a diminishing-returns rule:

1. broad offline evidence did not pass;
2. local research evidence was sparse and adverse at the available strict horizon;
3. short-run improvement coincided with reduced participation and saturation; and
4. recycling alternatives failed turnover and worst-case gates.

## Public interpretation rules

- Do not call any result “alpha,” “profitable,” or “outperforming.”
- Describe mark-to-mid as an open-inventory diagnostic, not realised profit.
- Describe all metrics as controlled-environment evidence.
- Preserve the adverse markouts, reduced participation, saturation, and rejected recycling designs when summarising the work.
